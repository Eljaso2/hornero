### CLM-053 — Adam Smith attacked chartered companies

**Newsletter Claim**: Adam Smith harshly attacked the role of these chartered companies.

**Updated Match Category**: ✓ CONFIRMED

**Web Sources**:
- Smith, Adam. *The Wealth of Nations* (1776), Book V, Chapter 1, Part III
- Smith, Adam. *The Wealth of Nations* (1776), Book IV, Chapter 7 ("Of Colonies")

**Key Quote**: "Exclusive companies are nuisances in every respect; always more or less inconvenient to the people whom they affect, and always more or less oppressive to the countries where they are established." (Book V, Ch. 1, Part III)

Smith specifically criticized the East India Company for mismanagement, oppression of local populations in India, and ultimately harming British interests. He argued that chartered companies with exclusive privileges were "incompetent managers" of trade due to the "negligence and profusion" problem, that they "oppress the inhabitants" of the countries where they trade, and that they are "injurious to the countries that establish them" by restricting trade and raising prices.

**Notes**: Fully confirmed. Smith's attack on chartered companies is one of the most well-established aspects of *The Wealth of Nations*. The newsletter's claim that Smith "atacó duramente el rol de estas compañías" is accurate. Smith's criticism was extensive and specifically targeted the East India Company and similar chartered monopolies in both Book IV Chapter 7 (Of Colonies) and Book V Chapter 1 Part III (on joint-stock companies). The phrase "nuisances in every respect" is a direct and harsh condemnation.
