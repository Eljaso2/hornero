# Research Overview — Tricontinental Newsletter Fact-Check

## Research Topic
Fact-check the Tricontinental Institute newsletter about AI in Latin America, verifying every factual claim against available research materials and web sources.

## Core Research Questions
1. Are the dates, names, and events referenced in the newsletter accurate?
2. Are direct quotes correctly attributed and accurately reproduced?
3. Are legislative references (laws, bills, articles) correctly cited?
4. Are numerical data (statistics, percentages, figures) correct?
5. Are historical claims and comparisons (VOC/EIC, La Forestal, Marx) accurate?
6. Are attributions (who said what, who did what) correct?

## Research Scope
- **Includes**: All factual claims in the newsletter text that can be verified externally
- **Excludes**: Purely rhetorical statements, opinions, editorial judgments, and narrative framing that cannot be factually verified

## Research Stance
- **Rigor over speed**: Better to have fewer verified claims than more uncertain ones
- **Source fidelity**: Always preserve exact text from sources, never paraphrase
- **Attribution precision**: When a quote has a chain of attribution (e.g., Marx citing Raffles), document the full chain
- **Nuance preservation**: When claims are partially correct, document what's right and what's wrong separately
- **No hallucination tolerance**: Any claim that cannot be verified must be clearly flagged, never confirmed by assumption
