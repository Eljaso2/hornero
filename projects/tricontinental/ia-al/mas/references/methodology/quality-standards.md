# Quality Standards — Newsletter Tricontinental IA en AL

## Writing Style: Militante-Académico

### Voice
- **Autoritative interpreter, not neutral observer**: You name power relations, identify who benefits, who loses. You don't pretend both sides are equivalent.
- **Politically committed, methodologically rigorous**: Position is explicit (soberanía digital), but evidence is required. No claim without source.
- **High certainty on well-sourced claims**: When evidence is strong, state it firmly. "Argentina cede soberanía digital a Palantir" — not "podría ceder".

### Tone
- **Muscular sentences, not academic jargon**: Short, precise, forceful. Avoid passive constructions that obscure agency.
- **Vivid political language**: "imperialismo digital", "dato-exportismo", "colonialismo de datos" — these are analytical categories, not propaganda slogans.
- **Scholarly caution where evidence is incomplete**: When a claim is plausible but not fully documented, note it. "Thiel habría participado en la redacción según la oposición, pero no hay prueba directa" — honest attribution.

### Forbidden Language
- **Neoliberal euphemisms unchallenged**: "innovación", "disrupción", "eficiencia" used as self-evident positives. These words always need critique — who innovates? who is disrupted? whose efficiency?
- **Hedging on sovereignty claims that are well-sourced**: Don't say "podría afectar soberanía" when the evidence says "afecta soberanía".
- **"Perhaps", "might" on documented facts**: If a source confirms it, say it. Reserve uncertainty for genuinely uncertain claims.
- **Passive constructions that hide agency**: "Los datos fueron cedidos" → "Milei cedió los datos". Name who acts.

### Audience
- **Tricontinental readers**: Latin American left, union organizers, social movements, academics.
- **Assume political literacy**: Readers understand imperialism, sovereignty, colonialism. Don't over-explain basics.
- **Don't assume technical literacy**: Explain RAG, DAO, LLM in brief context. Readers are politically sophisticated but not necessarily tech-savvy.

## Content Quality Standards

### Sourcing (QUA-03 Verifiable Data Lineage)
- **Every factual claim must have a [SRC-XXX] citation**: No exceptions.
- **Direct quotes must have attribution**: Author, publication, date.
- **No "widely known" or "reportedly" without source**: If you can't source it, don't claim it.
- **Mark unverified claims explicitly**: "Según la oposición [SRC-XXX], aunque no hay prueba independiente..."

### Argument Quality
- **Sufficient evidence per argument**: Each key argument needs at least 2 supporting sources or 1 strong source with direct quote.
- **No logical leaps**: Don't jump from "Thiel met Milei" to "Thiel wrote the law" without evidence. Document what is confirmed, what is inferred, what is alleged.
- **Explicit distinction between fact and inference**: "Milei se reunió con Thiel (confirmado) → el proyecto reconoce DAOs que Thiel financió (confirmado) → Thiel participó en redacción (allegado por oposición, no confirmado)".

### Structural Quality
- **Progressive argument**: Each section adds a new dimension, not just more examples of the same point.
- **Transitions between sections**: Each section ends connecting to the next. Not abrupt jumps.
- **No duplicate content**: Same anecdote, quote, or data point should appear in one section only.
- **Consistent terminology**: "dato-exportador" always, not sometimes "data-exportador". "soberanía digital" always, not sometimes "digital sovereignty".

## Format Quality Standards

### Markdown Format
- Sections start with ## (never # inside sections)
- Blank line at beginning and end of each section file
- Blank lines before and after all block elements (lists, quotes, tables)
- Direct quotes in `>` block format
- Tables in standard Markdown format
- No raw HTML

### Citation Format
- In-text: [SRC-XXX]
- Direct quotes: `> "Quote text" — Author, Publication, Date`
- End references: Full expansion of all SRC-XXX with URL, date, credibility rating
